ONE MINISTRY
Livestock movement, export certification and inspection run every day. Each depends on who holds the position and whether they are still qualified to act.

Routine disease surveillance and reporting from local and subnational offices. Vaccination campaigns and cold chain. Movement control, quarantine and permit issue. Certification for export consignments. Inspection of food-producing animals and premises. Residue and antimicrobial use monitoring in livestock. Herd health, production loss and mortality investigation. Wildlife interface work where the mandate extends to it.

Every one of those has a position behind it. Ask who holds the responsible veterinary position in a given locality today. If the answer takes a round of phone calls, the roster was accurate on the day it was drawn up and has not been accountable to anyone since.

Competence is the same question in a different form. Officers are trained in sampling, inspection and certification under national and donor programmes. How does the service know the officer signing an export certificate this week is still qualified to sign it?

Almost none of this work is a single-service job. A residue detection needs the laboratory that runs the confirmatory method and the authority that can hold the consignment. A movement control decision needs the neighbouring country's counterpart, by name, today.

THE REALITY
A common international framework exists. National capacity is uneven. What neither provides is the collaboration between the institutions doing the work.

A shared framework already governs animal health worldwide: the World Organisation for Animal Health (WOAH) standards, the World Animal Health Information System (WAHIS) for disease notification, and the Performance of Veterinary Services (PVS) Pathway for capacity evaluation. What the framework does not produce is collaboration in the daily work.

Veterinary services range from well-resourced and accredited to thinly staffed, with a single district officer covering an area that takes a day to cross and case records kept on paper. Whatever a country’s level, the veterinary service, the laboratories, the district offices, the producers and the trade authority each do their part, and the collaboration between them depends on who happens to know whom.

The gap is not capacity. A well-resourced service feels it as fragmentation, a thinly resourced one as fragility, and in both it sits between institutions rather than inside any of them. Building capacity will not close it on its own. The collaboration between institutions needs a standing home: who holds each position today, which procedure is current, and whether a finding reached the people who must act on it. Where capacity is uneven, that same collaboration shows a district which laboratory can run the method it needs, and who to contact there.

THE COST
A PVS evaluation finds the gaps. What costs a country is how many are still open when the next event arrives.

The WAHIS clock runs for every significant disease event: detect it, verify it, notify within the required window. The delays behind a late or uncertain notification rarely sit inside one office. A sample waits on a laboratory. A report waits on a district. Trading partners answer slow or uncertain reporting with blunt, country-wide restrictions that outlast the event.

A Performance of Veterinary Services evaluation names the gaps in writing: emergency coordination, workforce continuity, laboratory linkage. Closing each depends on another office, another level or a partner doing its part, and following it up falls to whoever has time. The same gap appears in the next mission’s report.

Mortality is the smallest part of what an animal health event costs. An export channel can close overnight, and for a pastoralist household the herd is its savings. The veterinary service sees the deaths, the trade ministry the closed market, the finance ministry the fiscal hole. No one sees the whole while the event is running.

A Minister of Agriculture is asked how the livestock sector is doing against the country’s own WOAH obligations. Between PVS missions, that answer is assembled for the occasion, or taken from the last report.

The cost is counted in markets closed, herds lost, and the same gap found twice.

WHAT COLLABORATION REQUIRES
Four conditions decide whether a veterinary service can work one event together.

A memorandum between departments, a technical committee that meets each quarter, a contact list from the last PVS mission: all of it is real, and none of it decides what happens on the day a district veterinary officer needs a movement decision from a counterpart across the border. What decides it is whether four conditions hold.

01
Everyone with a role can be found today.

One directory of positions across district offices, veterinary laboratories, inspection, certification and campaign teams, each with a named owner who keeps it current. When a position falls vacant, the service knows the same day.

02
Everyone works to the same procedure.

One current version of each procedure for movement control, quarantine, sampling and export certification, with an owner and a review date, assigned to the positions that use it. Not a district copy two revisions old.

03
Everyone hears at once, and it can be shown.

When a notifiable disease is suspected, the alert reaches every position that must act, across email, text message and voice, with receipt confirmed for each one.

04
Every finding has an owner and closes with evidence.

A finding from an event, an exercise or a PVS mission goes to the position that holds the fix, in whichever office it sits, and closes on the evidence the fix produced.

The people in this work are not all government staff. The private veterinarian who saw the herd first, the abattoir, the producer association, the trader and the laboratory running the method each hold part of what an event needs. Each works in the role it is given, without access to the service’s national systems.

When the four hold, an event does not begin with a scramble. The people are known, the procedure is current, the alert has arrived, and the team has exercised on the same structure it will use for real.

THE MISSING PIECE
OACI is the collaboration layer that holds those four conditions, around the systems the service already runs.

OACI does not replace WAHIS, the national animal health information system, animal identification registries or the laboratory information system. They remain the systems of record. OACI holds what sits between them: who holds each position, which procedure is current, whether an alert was received, and whether a finding closed. What moves through it is the signal, and the proof that the signal is fit to act on.

OACI does not assess or score a country; WOAH does that. What OACI holds is the work of closing what the assessment found. Each gap goes to the position that can fix it, in whichever office or partner holds it, and stays open until the fix is proven. The director sees every open gap in one place, and the minister has an answer between missions rather than one assembled for the occasion.

It is the discipline laboratories know from accreditation, applied to the work between offices.

A veterinary service can use it on its own, across its own offices, before any other institution joins. The OACI Platform holds this layer on Government-Standard Security, with the government always the data controller. The OACI Platform page carries the security and integration detail.

The understanding behind it is old. What OACI adds is a home for it inside the service that carries it today.

Abattoir Findings That Reach the Field

A meat inspector on the abattoir line records condemnations every working day. When the inspection service sees an unusual pattern of condemned carcasses from one district, the alert reaches the field veterinary service for that district while there is still time to look at the herd.

Transboundary Disease

Peste des petits ruminants, foot-and-mouth disease and African swine fever are decided at borders. Each district knows its counterpart position across the border, and the joint movement and vaccination procedure is held in one current version. The 2030 target for eradicating peste des petits ruminants is a deadline every Minister of Agriculture already owns.

Vaccination Campaigns

Who holds each district’s campaign position, which cold-chain procedure is current, and which under-covered districts are still open are all known during the campaign, so a district is closed while the campaign is running, not discovered after it.

Producers and Private Veterinarians

Pastoralist associations, abattoirs, traders, private veterinarians and vaccine suppliers work on the same event and to the same procedure as the service, in the roles it gives them and without access to its national systems.

It turns preparedness from a series of episodic activities into a sustained operating rhythm.
Disease data stays where the country keeps it.

National systems stay in place and remain the systems of record. What moves between institutions is what the country chooses to share: alerts, indicators and the record of the work. Animal disease data is commercially and diplomatically sensitive, so the country decides what is shared, with whom and when. The government is always the data controller.

PROVEN AT NATIONAL SCALE
The way of working is proven. Africa does not pay to build it again.

The space between institutions has been run as infrastructure for more than twenty years, across a whole country’s agriculture, health and emergency agencies, through real events and real corrective actions. Veterinary, food and human laboratories kept their own authority and their own records, and the signal still moved between them.

No African country funds that build. OACI is an African-incorporated, African-controlled non-profit that adapts it. What a country contributes goes to adapting and running its own layer, and every country that joins lowers the cost for the others.

CROSS-DOMAIN RESPONSE
When the same event is also in people

Each ministry sees what the other cannot.

Unusual abortions in a district’s goats may matter to the clinic eleven kilometres away. An unexplained human cluster may change what the service investigates in the herds. Neither ministry is the other’s early warning. Each holds something the other needs.

When the country is ready, the health ministry joins the same layer with its own positions, procedures and findings, working the same event as a peer, not a guest.

HOW A COUNTRY BEGINS
A service starts with the work that hurts most.

An export certification backlog, a PVS gap that will not close, a vaccination campaign, a border corridor. The service names a position accountable for the directory, sets its first procedures and dates its first exercise.

What the first year produces is a measurement, not a verdict: which positions are filled, which procedures are current, which findings have closed. Improvement starts from there.

When the country is ready, the health ministry and the neighbouring service join the same layer. Cross-border work is the return on a decision made at home, not the price of entry. No second procurement, and no ministry becomes a guest in another’s system.

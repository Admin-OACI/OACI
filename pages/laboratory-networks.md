THE REALITY
The laboratories are there. What connects them, and proves them, is not yet in place.

Much of Africa has about one pathologist per million people. The United States has roughly forty. Every result a country relies on passes through a small number of qualified hands.

Each laboratory answers to the ministry that funds it: clinical benches to health, veterinary benches to livestock and agriculture, food laboratories to standards or agriculture, water laboratories to environment. Each keeps its own records, its own methods and its own quality system, often on paper.

The largest review of laboratory data on the continent read more than 800,000 records from 205 laboratories in fourteen countries. About twelve records in every hundred carried the information that makes a result usable beyond the bench that produced it.

Nothing between these laboratories tells a ministry which bench can run which test today, to which method, with which analyst qualified to sign it.

~1 vs ~40

Pathologists per million people, much of Africa and the United States. Fleming et al., African Journal of Laboratory Medicine, 2020.

THE COST
A result no one can stand behind is a confident wrong answer.

It clears a shipment that should have been held, or holds one that should have cleared. It confirms a health event late, or at a laboratory that was never validated for the method.

Accreditation is prepared for in the weeks before an assessor arrives. A corrective action opened after a proficiency failure has no owner by the next round.

When a senior analyst leaves, the method knowledge and the training record often leave too. The replacement starts from memory, not from a documented baseline.

A project pays for equipment, a mentor and a target. The bench reaches a rating. The grant ends, and nothing holds the capability in place. The next project buys the same capability again.

FOUR LABORATORY SYSTEMS
Four benches, four ministries, one country's health.
CLINICAL AND PUBLIC HEALTH
A diagnosis is as sound as the bench behind it.

Bacteriology, virology, parasitology, serology, molecular. National reference to district. ISO 15189 and the stepwise path toward it.

Which analysts at the regional laboratory are qualified today for the molecular method the reference laboratory validated last year?

A cluster of unexplained fever needs the reference laboratory, the district bench that took the samples and the surveillance officer on one thread before the next samples travel.

VETERINARY
A herd is cleared or held on the strength of one result.

Veterinary diagnostics, pathology, serology, residues. Results that carry the country's animal health reporting and its livestock trade. ISO/IEC 17025.

Is the central veterinary laboratory's proficiency current for that method, and who signed off the last round?

A notifiable disease confirmed at a market needs the veterinary laboratory, veterinary services and public health to share the result before animals move again.

FOOD
A shipment is released on a result a trading partner will check.

Microbiology, residues, mycotoxins, contaminants. Methods recognised in trade. ISO/IEC 17025.

Does the laboratory issuing export results still hold accreditation in scope for aflatoxin, and is the analyst signing them certified?

A residue finding on export produce has to reach the certifying officer, the grower and trade before the next consignment ships.

ENVIRONMENTAL
A water source reopens when the result says it is safe.

Water bacteriology and chemistry, soil, air. ISO/IEC 17025.

Which laboratory can confirm cholera in a supply this week, using the method the ministry approved?

A contaminated source needs the water laboratory, environmental health and the clinical laboratory seeing cases working one health event.

WHY THEY ARE ONE SYSTEM
The sample arrives in one laboratory. The answer needs several.
SIGNAL OUTWARD

The same pathogen can arrive in a patient, an animal, a food and a water source in one week. A veterinary result explains a human cluster; a food result explains both; the water result closes the event. None is another laboratory's early warning. Each holds part of the answer.

RESOURCE INWARD

A sound bench draws validated methods, current SOPs and proficiency practice from like-kind laboratories. A laboratory island does not improve, however well it measures itself.

BENCH BEFORE NETWORK

The network is unreachable until each bench can be trusted. Federating results no one can stand behind produces shared uncertainty, not shared intelligence. So the work starts at the bench.

WHAT COLLABORATION REQUIRES
Collaboration is a working condition, not a relationship.

A memorandum between ministries, a laboratory working group that meets each quarter, a workshop that ends in a contact list. All of it is real, and none of it changes what happens on the day a district bench needs a confirmation from a reference laboratory it has never worked with.

01
Every laboratory position can be found today.

Positions, methods and capability across all four laboratory systems, each with a named owner. When a position falls vacant, the system knows the same day.

02
Every bench works to the current version.

The current version is the current version: owned, dated, and the same for everyone who touches it.

03
Competence is shown, not assumed.

Proficiency per analyst and per method, training tied to competence rather than attendance, and the certificate updating the position.

04
Every finding has an owner and closes with evidence.

A corrective action linked to the result that raised it, closed on evidence an assessor can follow.

Quality has four legs, held per laboratory: accreditation in scope and current, proficiency performance, method capability and scope, and equipment in service. Beside them sits one more question: is each position filled by someone qualified today. OACI holds these against the country's own standards. It does not assess or score a country, and it does not issue accreditation. The accreditation body does.

THE MISSING PIECE
The space between laboratories has no owner.

Each laboratory is funded for its own mandate. An appropriation is scoped to a ministry. A grant has a host, a scope and an end date. The work between laboratories, shared methods, referral, proficiency across domains, joint response, sits in no one's budget.

A LIMS records results. An interface moves them between systems. Neither makes sure someone qualified reads a result against what another laboratory holds, acts on it, and proves the action closed. That is the collaboration layer.

The OACI Platform gives that space a standing collaboration layer without replacing the systems beneath it. Results stay in the laboratory's own LIMS or national system. What moves through the layer is the signal and the proof that it is fit to act on.

A single laboratory can use it in its own work before a second laboratory joins.

Collaboration crosses borders. Laboratory records never do.

Results and sample data stay on national systems, with the government always the data controller.

The OACI Platform holds this layer on Government-Standard Security. The OACI Platform page carries the security, sovereignty and integration detail.

No African country funds the build. What a country contributes goes to adapting and running it, and every country that joins lowers the cost for the others.

PROVEN AT NATIONAL SCALE
It began with laboratories.

The collaboration technology OACI brings to Africa began with laboratories. A laboratory network pilot in 2002 proved the concept, and that record earned the funding that extended it to wider food protection and One Health work. For more than 20 years since, laboratory networks have run on it across human, animal, food and environmental laboratories, outlasting the grants, leadership changes and priorities that end most programmes.

What those years proved is the loop, not the software. A proficiency round or an exercise reveals a gap. The gap gets a name, an owner and a date. If it is competence, training runs and the certificate updates the position. The next round tests whether the fix held.

CAPABILITIES
Twelve conditions carry the work, from one bench to a national network.
AT THE BENCH: TRUST IN THE RESULT
FROM COLLECTION TO REPORT
Where is that sample right now, and who holds it?

Sample collection scheduled and assigned by position. Each sample logged in once at receipt and tracked through analysis to the final report under chain of custody. Deadlines and sample status raise alerts before a turnaround target is missed.

QUALITY SYSTEM, LIVE
When did the last corrective action close, and on what evidence?

Each corrective action linked to the result that raised it and traceable to source. Calibration and maintenance scheduled. Alerts when quality trends signal risk.

ONE CURRENT VERSION
Is every analyst running the SOP revision you approved?

SOPs and methods version-controlled with named owners, acknowledged by every position assigned to them, revisions traceable across sites. Past corrective actions stay searchable when the same problem returns.

LEARNING MANAGEMENT
Which analysts are qualified for this method today?

A built-in learning management system: role-specific, SCORM-compliant courses tied to accreditation requirements, with assessments and certificates issued on completion and tracked to expiry. Proficiency results feed competence records. An out-of-compliance result assigns targeted training, and the certificate closes the loop. Training delivered elsewhere is recorded against the same position.

ACCREDITATION DASHBOARD
If an assessor arrived tomorrow, what would they find?

A live view against ISO/IEC 17025 or ISO 15189, per method: scope and expiry dates, open non-conformances, proficiency results, equipment in service and analyst qualifications. Expiry alerts fire before a lapse. Readiness is a current condition, not a pre-audit scramble.

ANY STARTING POINT
Does a laboratory need a LIMS to begin?

The layer works above the LIMS a laboratory already runs, and across several. Laboratories on paper begin with electronic data capture. Results sent securely to regulators.

INCIDENT RESPONSE
What happens in the hour after an anomalous result?

The result opens an incident workflow, alerts the right positions, delegates tasks by role, links to corrective action and leaves an audit log.

ONE WORKSPACE WITH ITS REGULATORS
Where does the laboratory work with its ministry and regulators?

The laboratory, its ministry and its regulators in one encrypted workspace with role-based access: version-controlled documents, meetings, and tasks tracked to closure.

ACROSS THE NETWORK: TRUST BETWEEN INSTITUTIONS
A LABORATORY DIRECTORY
Which laboratory can run this test, to this method, this week?

Capability profiles for every laboratory the country connects: methods and scope, accreditation, equipment in service, qualified analysts. Validated at a rhythm each institution sets.

PROFICIENCY ACROSS THE NETWORK
Which laboratories can be trusted for this test before it is needed?

Proficiency rounds scheduled and tracked per laboratory, per analyst and per method over time. The network's quality-levelling mechanism, not an administrative task.

WORKGROUPS FOR THREE MOMENTS
When several laboratories must work one problem, where does the work happen?

Secure workgroups for a health event (human, animal and environmental benches on one event), a recall (food or water laboratory, ministry and reference laboratory on one thread) and internal SOP development. Documents, meetings and tasks tracked to closure.

STANDING RESPONSE TEAMS
When a result is needed under pressure, is the laboratory response stood up, or assembled?

Laboratory positions on every standing response team, drawn from clinical, veterinary, food and environmental laboratories, with referral routes and methods agreed in advance. Each position is named, trained with the team and exercised on the procedures it will use for real. The laboratory side starts from a capability assessment that names its gaps. After every activation or exercise, an after-action report and improvement plan follow, each action with an owner and a deadline, re-tested in the next exercise.

HOW A COUNTRY BEGINS
A country begins where its laboratories already are.
A LABORATORY WITH A LIMS

Connect it. The quality, training and corrective-action layer sits above the system it already runs.

A LABORATORY ON PAPER

Start with electronic data capture, document control and the training record. No LIMS is required to begin.

A LABORATORY PREPARING FOR ACCREDITATION

The four legs of quality held live, so readiness for ISO/IEC 17025 or ISO 15189 is a current condition, not a pre-audit scramble.

Deeper.

From the national reference laboratory to regional and district benches, and to the university, mission and private laboratories the country chooses to include.

Wider.

From laboratories into the solutions they serve: public health, animal health, food safety, food security, environmental health and emergency response. The standing response team a country builds for its first entry point is the same team every later solution activates, with new domains joining as their positions are filled.

Across.

Once the national network is sound, the country chooses what to share with neighbours and regional laboratory networks: the alerts and indicators collaboration needs, never the underlying records.

Laboratory networks are the backbone of health security.

Connect

Link public health, veterinary, food safety, and research laboratories into a unified network with shared protocols, secure communication channels, and coordinated workflows across institutions and borders.

Standardize

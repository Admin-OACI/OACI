One Africa Collaboration Initiative

Unifying One Health for Africa
Through Intentional Collaboration
OVERVIEW

One Africa Collaboration Initiative (OACI) is an African-governed body established to hold a role no single actor on the continent currently holds: the permanent collaboration of One Health across all 55 nations. We are not a vendor selling a system, and not a project that ends when a grant does. We exist to make African health systems work together, within borders and across them, and to keep doing it long after any one funding cycle.

Intentional collaboration is the infrastructure, not the emergency response. It is designed in advance, sustained across cycles, structured to survive personnel changes and political shifts. It does not depend on who happens to pick up the phone.

We built OACI because the continent’s hardest health failures are failures of collaboration, not of capability. Across the continent, the mandates are in place. What is missing is the ability to act on them. Many nations lack the trained people, the people in the right roles, the functioning laboratories, and the resources to turn a mandate into a working system. Where solutions do exist, they are too often projects rather than sustained capability.

Ebola, mpox, Rift Valley fever, avian influenza, and repeated food and environmental emergencies have shown the cost of this gap. Human, animal, plant, and environmental health are a single connected problem, yet the systems meant to manage them still work in isolation. What has been missing is the collaboration layer that lets them act as one, and that survives past the end of a program. That is what we are here to build.

Why Now

The mandate for this collaboration already exists: the African Union’s Agenda 2063 and its One Health, food safety, and health strategies, Africa CDC’s continental frameworks, the Regional Economic Communities, and the international standards and health regulations all call for it. The tools to deliver it are proven and ready. What has been missing is a group willing to build the permanent layer that connects them, an African-governed framework that survives past any single project.

Acting now is the reasonable response to a gap that is already defined, already funded in pieces, and already costing the continent in every outbreak that crosses a border before anyone has the full picture. The alternative is to keep waiting, when no existing actor has yet built the integrated collaboration framework that OACI now makes possible.

OACI is governed in Africa, by African professionals with in-country expertise, and it is a not-for-profit initiative with no shareholders and no equity. No one profits from a government’s participation. What OACI earns is returned to the mission: sustaining the platform, strengthening national capacity, and reaching more nations. The money follows the mandate, not a margin. Each nation keeps ownership of its own data; national records stay on national systems, and the platform shows only what a government chooses to share.

OACI connects the institutions whose strategies already align but whose operations still run separately; it does not replace them. It works alongside Africa CDC, the African Union, WHO, FAO, WOAH, and each country’s own systems, giving their frameworks an operational platform that links to what already exists rather than competing with it. It is delivered with African partners in laboratory design, food safety audit, and standards and accreditation. Existing donor and African Union investment can be partnered with OACI to extend what already works into the next country, rather than build it from scratch each time. And because capability is measured continuously rather than assessed periodically, a partner can see what an investment produced, not only what it paid for.

OACI exists to turn One Health from a goal on a page into a sustainable solution: capability a country can define, measure, and improve, and keep running long after the projects that started it have ended.

MISSION
Giving institutions the means to act as one

OACI gives the institutions One Health depends on the means to act as one, within borders and across them.

Bringing the mission to life: how ministries, laboratories, and agencies collaborate through OACI

Across the continent, the mandates for One Health are already in place. What is missing is the ability to act on them: the right people at the table, the functioning laboratories, the shared systems, and the sustained capability to turn a mandate into working practice. OACI provides that missing layer. It brings ministries of health, agriculture, environment, and veterinary services onto shared workflows with the laboratories and response agencies they depend on, so a signal seen in one place reaches everyone who needs it in time to act.

OACI is built to last. It is African-governed and not-for-profit, designed to sustain coordination long after the projects that started it have ended, and to carry what works in one nation across borders to the next. Working alongside Africa CDC, the African Union, WHO, FAO, WOAH, and each country’s own systems, OACI gives their frameworks the operational platform to run on, so One Health becomes something every nation can define, measure, and sustain.

VISION

OACI envisions an Africa where governments, laboratories, universities, industry, and partners collaborate through a One Health approach to protect the health of people, animals, plants, and the environment. Where nations prevent, detect, and respond together, within and across borders, and keep advancing their capability.

To Achieve This Vision

OACI builds on a proven system and framework currently used by the United States. Over more than twenty years, the US has integrated health, agriculture, environmental, and homeland security agencies across all 50 states into a true national system. OACI has adapted this capability to enable Africa to collaborate on One Health within each nation and across the continent.

Because effective collaboration requires more than communication, OACI transforms the internationally recognized frameworks countries can use to assess their readiness, such as the FAO/WHO Food Control System Assessment Tool for food safety and OH-APP for One Health, from static spreadsheets into dynamic, evidence-based management systems. Instead of periodic assessments that produce reports and sit on a shelf, OACI enables countries to continuously define, measure, manage, and improve their capabilities, identifying the gaps that guide strategic investment in capacity building, training, and exercises before an emergency strikes.

Each country runs its own system on a secure collaborative platform, and national records never leave national servers. That system provides tailored dashboards, real-time KPIs, capability maturity scores, evidence management, automated workflows, and role-based accountability. By integrating collaboration, assessments, and operational information into a single platform, OACI creates shared situational awareness and a rapid response capability, delivering the right information to the right people at the right time. This also reduces redundant data entry and eliminates the information silos that delay response and keep the right people from acting in time.

The result: stronger preparedness, smarter investment where the gaps actually are, and faster, more coordinated decisions. Countries see emerging risks earlier, respond more effectively, and keep improving health outcomes across human, animal, plant, and environmental health.

If you can’t define One Health, you can’t measure it. If you can’t measure it, you can’t manage it. If you can’t manage it, you can’t improve it.
OUR VALUES
Principles that guide everything we do
VALUE 01
African-owned and African-led

The initiative is governed in Africa, by African professionals, so the capability, the data, and the decisions stay with the nations it serves.

The hand that writes the story should be the hand that lives it. OACI is governed by Africa, for Africa, and answers to no one but the nations it serves.

VALUE 02
Accountability through measurement

A mandate means nothing until it is defined, measured, and improved. OACI holds itself and its partners to the same standard: results that can be shown, not just promised.

A carpenter who does not measure wastes the wood and builds what will not stand. OACI provides the ruler that turns One Health from a goal into measured achievement.

VALUE 03
Mission before margin

OACI is not-for-profit by design. No one profits from a government’s participation; what it earns is returned to sustaining and extending the mission.

The coin sits light on the scale. The health of a village is heavier, and what OACI earns serves the side that matters: One Health for Africa.

VALUE 04
Built to last

OACI exists to outlive projects, grants, and funding cycles, because health coordination that disappears when the money ends is not coordination at all.

The baobab carries its own water. When the rains fail, it does not wither and it does not leave. Strip its bark and it grows new bark over the wound. Fire passes and it stands. What is built to last is not what avoids hard seasons, but what is made to survive them.

VALUE 05
Collaboration over control

OACI connects institutions rather than replacing them, bringing the right people to the table and working alongside existing bodies rather than competing with them.

A single reed breaks in the hand. A thousand woven together carry the harvest home. OACI does not replace what already exists; it weaves it stronger.

VALUE 06
Integrity earned through proof

Trust is built by delivering and by measuring honestly, showing what works and where the gaps remain, so every claim OACI makes is one it can stand behind.

A finished clay pot is tapped before it is trusted — it rings clear where it is whole, and dull where it is cracked. The sound cannot be bought or argued with. OACI lets its work be struck and heard, because integrity that can be tested never has to be defended.

VALUE 07
Service to African health and wellbeing

Every part of the initiative exists for one end: better health outcomes for the people of Africa, across human, animal, plant, and environmental health.

A village well belongs to no single household, everyone drinks from it: the elder, the child, the stranger passing through. None is turned away. OACI exists for the same reason: One Health for every life, no exceptions.

MEET THE TEAM

OACI is led by African professionals with deep expertise in public health, veterinary medicine, food safety, environmental science, and laboratory systems. Our team brings decades of combined experience working within national governments, regional bodies, and international institutions across the continent.

We are not consultants who fly in and out. We are practitioners who have built systems, managed outbreaks, and negotiated the politics of cross-border coordination. Every member of the team has sat in the rooms where decisions are made and understands what it takes to turn a mandate into working practice.

Our leadership structure includes a Secretariat based in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, working closely with regional coordinators, national focal points, and technical advisors embedded across the African Union’s five regions. This distributed structure ensures that OACI’s work is informed by local realities and grounded in the day-to-day challenges that ministries and agencies face.

We are actively recruiting additional technical leads in laboratory networks, food safety auditing, emergency response coordination, and digital platform operations. If you are an African professional with deep in-country expertise and a commitment to One Health, we want to hear from you.

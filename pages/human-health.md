Human Health

Communities across Africa long understood that the health of a family depends on everything around it. Today a Ministry of Health carries that mandate through many departments, each doing its part.

OACI is the collaboration layer that keeps what the ministry has built working together and current, inside the ministry first and across ministries when the country is ready.

ONE MINISTRY
Every health event tests the routine work that came before it.

Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response defines how a country detects, reports, investigates and responds from the local level to the national level. Alongside it run event-based surveillance, screening at points of entry, immunization coverage and cold chain, antimicrobial resistance sentinel sites, facility licensing and inspection, foodborne investigation, water quality, chemical exposure and vector control.

Every one of those has a focal point. Ask who holds the surveillance focal point in a given locality today. If the answer takes a round of phone calls, the list was accurate on the day of the last workshop and has not been accountable to anyone since.

Competence is the same question in a different form. Thousands of officers are trained under national and donor programmes. How does the ministry know the officer on duty this week is still certified for the duty they are performing?

Almost none of this work is a single-ministry job. A foodborne investigation needs the laboratory that confirms the isolate and the inspector who can reach the premises. A chemical exposure needs the environmental sample and the regulator who holds the permit. Most often it needs the veterinary service, which is running the same problem on its own side.

THE REALITY
The investment is real. Keeping it current is the harder work.

Ministries of health have built real capacity, some over decades and some one programme at a time: emergency operations centres, upgraded laboratories, field epidemiology training, surge teams, procedures rewritten after the last event. Health information systems already connect much of it.

All of it is perishable. A procedure begins ageing the day it is signed. Trained officers transfer and rotate. Surge rosters go stale between exercises. An operations centre gets staffed by someone inheriting a desk rather than a position with its history.

Assessments see this at intervals. A Joint External Evaluation captures the country in the week of the mission, and the next one comes years later. What happens in between decides whether the capacity is there when the next event arrives.

Each department keeps its own part current as best it can. What belongs to no one is the whole: whether the roster, the procedure, the trained officer and the laboratory are all still ready at the same time. That needs a standing home of its own, shared by the departments that depend on it.

THE COST
The gaps get found. What costs a country is how many are still open when the next event arrives.

The 7-1-7 target (seven days to detect a health event, one to notify, seven to respond), the International Health Regulations and the Joint External Evaluation all measure the ministry as one system, on the clock. The delays they expose rarely sit inside one department. A delay found by surveillance sits in the laboratory. A delay in response sits in the district.

Every review ends in corrective actions, and every action plan lists activities. Each depends on another department, another level or another partner doing its part, and following it up falls to whoever has time. Findings stay in progress through one assessment and reappear in the next.

Leadership sees the result at intervals. Asked today how ready the ministry is, a director-general is given the last assessment's answer, or a new one assembled for the occasion.

Each donor asks for its own report on the same work. Laboratories, private providers and universities hold part of what an event needs, and are usually reached during the emergency rather than worked with before it.

The cost is counted in days lost on the 7-1-7 clock, and in the same gap found twice.

WHAT COLLABORATION REQUIRES
Four conditions decide whether a ministry can work one event together.

A memorandum between departments, a technical working group that meets each quarter, a contact list from the last workshop: all of it is real, and none of it decides what happens on the day a district surveillance officer needs a confirmed result from a laboratory she has never worked with. What decides it is whether four conditions hold.

01
Everyone with a role can be found today.

One directory of positions across surveillance, laboratories, the operations centre, immunization and the districts, each with a named owner who keeps it current. When a position falls vacant, the ministry knows the same day.

02
Everyone works to the same procedure.

One current version of each joint procedure, with an owner and a review date, assigned to the positions that use it. Not a district copy two revisions old.

03
Everyone hears at once, and it can be shown.

An alert reaches every position that must act, across email, text message and voice, with receipt confirmed for each one.

04
Every finding has an owner and closes with evidence.

A finding from an event, an exercise, a 7-1-7 review or an assessment goes to the position that holds the fix, in whichever department it sits, and closes on the evidence the fix produced.

The people in this work are not all ministry staff. The laboratory confirming the isolate, the hospital reporting the cluster, the private practitioner and the university running the method each hold part of what an event needs. Each works in the role it is given, without access to the ministry's national systems.

When the four hold, an event does not begin with a scramble. The people are known, the procedure is current, the alert has arrived, and the team has exercised on the same structure it will use for real.

THE MISSING PIECE
OACI is the collaboration layer that holds those four conditions, around the systems the ministry already runs.

OACI does not replace DHIS2, the national health record or the laboratory information system. They remain the systems of record. OACI holds what sits between them: who holds each position, which procedure is current, whether an alert was received, and whether a finding closed. What moves through it is the signal, and the proof that the signal is fit to act on.

The last health event found the gaps. What matters is whether the next one finds the same gaps. With OACI, each gap goes to the person who can fix it, in whichever department or partner holds it, and stays open until the fix is proven. Leadership sees every open gap in one place: which are closing, which are stuck, and which keep coming back.

It is the discipline laboratories know from accreditation, applied to the work between departments.

A ministry can use it on its own, across its own departments, before any other institution joins. The OACI Platform holds this layer on Government-Standard Security, with the government always the data controller. The OACI Platform page carries the security and integration detail.

The understanding behind it is old. What OACI adds is a home for it inside the institutions that now carry it.

Emergency Operations Centre

The surge roster names who is on it this month and whether each person is still qualified. When the centre activates, the alert reaches every position on the roster and each receipt is recorded.

Antimicrobial Resistance

Every activity in the national action plan has an owner, in the ministry, the laboratories or the hospitals, and stays open until the evidence shows it is done. Progress is known before the next review asks.

Immunization Campaigns

Who holds each district's campaign position, which cold-chain procedure is current, and which under-covered districts are still open are all known during the campaign, not after it.

Assessment Follow-Through

The priority actions from the last Joint External Evaluation and every 7-1-7 review, each assigned to the position that holds the fix, with a deadline and closure on evidence. The next assessment finds the record already there.

It turns preparedness from a series of episodic activities into a sustained operating rhythm.
National records stay where the country keeps them.

National systems stay in place and remain the systems of record. What moves between institutions is what the country chooses to share: alerts, indicators and the record of the work. The government is always the data controller.

PROVEN AT NATIONAL SCALE
The way of working is proven. Africa does not pay to build it again.

The space between institutions has been run as infrastructure for more than twenty years, across a whole country's health, agriculture and emergency agencies, through real events and real corrective actions. Laboratories kept their own authority and their own records, and the signal still moved between them.

No African country funds that build. OACI is an African-incorporated, African-controlled non-profit that adapts it. What a country contributes goes to adapting and running its own layer, and every country that joins lowers the cost for the others.

CROSS-DOMAIN RESPONSE
When the same event is also in animals

Each ministry sees what the other cannot.

A cluster the clinic cannot explain may already be visible in the district's herds. An unusual animal finding may change what health workers look for. Neither ministry is the other's early warning. Each holds something the other needs.

When the country is ready, the veterinary service joins the same layer with its own positions, procedures and findings, working the same event as a peer, not a guest.

HOW A COUNTRY BEGINS
A ministry starts with the work that hurts most.

The operations centre, the antimicrobial resistance plan, an immunization campaign, or the assessment items that will not close. The ministry names a position accountable for the directory, sets its first procedures and dates its first exercise.

What the first year produces is a measurement, not a verdict: which positions are filled, which procedures are current, which findings have closed. Improvement starts from there.

When the country is ready, the veterinary service and other institutions join the same layer. No second procurement, and no ministry becomes a guest in another's system.

Communities across Africa have long read the health of people and their animals as one. Today they sit in separate ministries, each seeing part of a public health event and neither seeing the whole. OACI is the layer that lets each institution act on what the others hold.

The country's own systems do the detecting. Collaboration is what turns that signal into action across both mandates.

THE REALITY
Detection capacity varies across Africa. Whether human health and animal health can act on each other's signals varies more.

Detection capacity is unevenly distributed across the fifty-five member states. Some nations have built substantial surveillance and laboratory infrastructure. Others rely on donor-funded programmes that end when the funding does. That is true in human health and in animal health, and the two are rarely at the same level in the same country.

Each carries its own consequence for failing. In human health it is measured in illness and death. In animal health it is measured in herd loss and in markets that close to the country's livestock. Different ministries, different budgets, different assessment cycles, and neither one is subordinate to the other.

Both are evaluated: the Joint External Evaluation on one side, the PVS Pathway on the other. Neither evaluation establishes whether they can work the same event together, or whether the laboratories, producers, private practitioners and universities that hold part of the picture have any route into the work.

That capability sits between two mandates, so neither ministry owns it, neither budget funds it, and neither assessment scores it.

THE COST
Much of what an event costs is counted. Adding it up is nobody's job.

The figures exist and they are accurate. Health has the patients. Veterinary services have the animals and the production loss. Agriculture has the crop. Trade has the rejected consignment. What no institution has ever been assigned is the job of putting them beside each other while the event is still running.

The 7-1-7 target, seven days to detect a health event, one to notify and seven to respond, came from human health, and it is the clearest statement anyone has made of what timeliness means in practice. Its origin is not a limitation. The three intervals describe any event that has to be found, reported and acted on, and both mandates already run against a notification clock: the International Health Regulations for human health, notification to the World Organisation for Animal Health for animal health. What differs is the obligation each reports to, not the shape of the measure.

The interval nobody reports is the one between them. A veterinary finding that takes four days to reach the health ministry has spent most of the response window before the response began, and that time appears in neither ministry's figures.

None of that argues for spending less on the diseases killing people now. It argues that the institutions holding the pieces have to be working the same event, not reporting on it separately. That is what collaboration means in practice, and it is what the One Africa Collaboration Initiative exists to operate.

HUMAN HEALTH
HUMAN HEALTH FOCUS
Every health event tests the routine work that came before it.

Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response defines how a country detects, reports, investigates and responds from the local level to the national level. Alongside it run event-based surveillance, screening at points of entry, immunization coverage and cold chain, antimicrobial resistance sentinel sites, facility licensing and inspection, foodborne investigation, water quality, chemical exposure and vector control.

Every one of those has a focal point. Ask who holds the surveillance focal point in a given locality today. If the answer takes a round of phone calls, the list was accurate on the day of the last workshop and has not been accountable to anyone since.

Competence is the same question in a different form. Thousands of officers are trained under national and donor programmes. How does the ministry know the officer on duty this week is still certified for the duty they are performing?

Almost none of this work is a single-ministry job. A foodborne investigation needs the laboratory that confirms the isolate and the inspector who can reach the premises. A chemical exposure needs the environmental sample and the regulator who holds the permit. Most often it needs the veterinary service, which is running the same problem on its own side.

ANIMAL HEALTH
ANIMAL HEALTH FOCUS
Livestock movement, export certification and inspection run every day. Each depends on who holds the position and whether they are still qualified to act.

Routine disease surveillance and reporting from local and subnational offices. Vaccination campaigns and cold chain. Movement control, quarantine and permit issue. Certification for export consignments. Inspection of food-producing animals and premises. Residue and antimicrobial use monitoring in livestock. Herd health, production loss and mortality investigation. Wildlife interface work where the mandate extends to it.

Every one of those has a position behind it. Ask who holds the responsible veterinary position in a given locality today. If the answer takes a round of phone calls, the roster was accurate on the day it was drawn up and has not been accountable to anyone since.

Competence is the same question in a different form. Officers are trained in sampling, inspection and certification under national and donor programmes. How does the service know the officer signing an export certificate this week is still qualified to sign it?

Almost none of this work is a single-service job. A residue detection needs the laboratory that runs the confirmatory method and the authority that can hold the consignment. A movement control decision needs the neighbouring country's counterpart, by name, today.

WHY THEY ARE ONE SYSTEM
Two mandates, one event. The signal arrives in one mandate and the response needs both.

A herder knew that what moved through the animals would move through the household, because both were in view from the same place. Today the animals are one ministry's responsibility and the household another's.

The traffic runs both ways. A veterinary service confirming an event needs the health ministry to know before people are exposed handling carcasses. A health ministry investigating an unexplained cluster needs the veterinary service to say what has been moving through the district's herds. Neither is the other's early warning system. Each holds something the other cannot see.

What one institution knows changes what the other should do. An unusual animal event can change what health workers look for, where they look and which exposures matter. An unexplained human cluster can change what veterinary services investigate in animals. The value is not that information crosses a ministry boundary. It is that each institution acts differently because it can see what the other knows.

What fails is not detection. It is the step after it. Reaching the other service depends on someone deciding to make a call, knowing who to call, and that person still holding the position. Ask whether last month's alert reached the service it was meant for, and where the record of it being sent is kept. That step is where a veterinary finding loses four days. The connection that closes it has to exist before the event, while there is still time to change the outcome.

WHAT COLLABORATION REQUIRES
Collaboration is a working condition, not a relationship.

Most of what gets called collaboration is a relationship. A memorandum signed by two ministers. A committee that meets each quarter. A workshop that ends in a contact list and a photograph. All of it is real, and none of it changes what happens on the Tuesday a district officer needs an answer from a service she has never worked with.

The operational sense is narrower and harder. Two institutions working the same event, each bringing what only they can see, under the same procedure, while each keeps its own mandate entirely. Not one reporting to the other. Not one hosting the other. Both inside the same work, at the same time, each answerable to its own minister.

The distinction decides outcomes.

The finding does not assemble itself. Epidemiology is the work of reading one record against another and recognising a pattern, and someone has to do it: see unusual abortions in a district and ask the clinic eleven kilometres away what has been walking through the door. That question is the event. Everything below exists so that when it is asked, the counterpart needed can be found, is working to the same procedure, hears at once, and what comes back is recorded where it will still be found when the people change.

01
Everyone with a role can be found today.

Not a list accurate on the morning of the last workshop, but a directory of positions across both ministries, each with a named owner accountable for keeping it current. When a position falls vacant, the system knows the same day.

02
Everyone is working to the same procedure.

One version of the shared investigation procedure, assigned to positions in both ministries, with an owner, a review date and an audit trail on every change. Not two documents that disagree at the moment they are needed.

03
Everyone hears at once, and it can be shown.

A signal reaches both ministries at the same time, addressed to positions rather than to a list of names, across email, SMS and voice, with delivery confirmed for each recipient. Not forwarded by whoever thought to forward it.

04
Every finding has an owner and closes with evidence.

A finding from an event or an exercise is assigned to a position in either ministry, carries a deadline, and closes only on evidence both ministries can read. Not marked done because the date passed.

None of that is a reporting line. It is a working group, and the people in it are not only civil servants. The laboratory that confirms the isolate, the private practitioner who saw the herd first, the abattoir, the producer association, the university running the confirmatory method. Each holds part of what the event requires, and each needs a route into the work rather than a place on a circulation list. They participate inside the layer, in the roles they are given, without access to either ministry's national systems.

When those four hold, an event does not begin with a scramble. The people with a role are known. The procedure they will work to is current. The alert reaches all of them at once. They have exercised together on the same structure they will use for real. A team is stood up rather than assembled, and what varies is the event, not what the team needs from underneath it.

That is the difference between two ministries notifying each other and two ministries working the same event.

THE MISSING PIECE
What is missing is infrastructure for the collaboration itself.

Some countries have built strong systems inside each mandate. Many are still building them, one programme at a time. In both, investment rightly builds what each mandate needs for its own work. The work between mandates can still depend on an agreement, a meeting, and the people who happen to know one another. A country still building can give that work a home from the start rather than add it later.

Neither ministry can reasonably build that space alone. Whoever builds it funds it, and the other would be planning its own response around a budget line, a leadership and an appropriation it does not control. A ministry that declines is being prudent.

And no funding instrument fits it. An appropriation is scoped to a mandate. A grant is scoped to its funder, hosted by one party, and ends on a date. Neither can be scoped to the space between two mandates, because no single funder owns that space. It is also why neither mandate can be the destination for the other's findings. A layer that lives inside one of them is that ministry's system, and the other is a guest in it.

The OACI Platform gives that space a standing operating layer, without replacing the systems beneath it. Each ministry joins as a peer, and participation does not end when a grant does. Surveillance, laboratory, clinical and veterinary records stay on national systems, which remain the systems of record. The layer reads from them and feeds back to them, and what moves through it is alerts, indicators and the record of the work.

No ministry funds the build. A country inherits more than two decades of continuous government operation: real events, real corrective actions, and a system shaped by the officials who had to work together through them, all paid for before any African country was asked to consider it.

What a country contributes goes to running it, not building it. A ministry can use the layer in its own work before a second institution joins.

Readiness becomes something a country can see today, rather than reconstruct from the last external assessment.

The OACI Platform holds this layer on Government-Standard Security, with the government always the data controller. The OACI Platform page carries the security, sovereignty and integration detail.

CAPABILITIES
Eight conditions carry the work, whatever the event turns out to be.

Collaboration fails in the same few ways whether the trigger is an infectious disease, a chemical release or contaminated food, and each is visible long before anything happens.

DELIVERY CONFIRMED
Did last month's alert actually reach anyone?

Alerts reach the positions that need them in both ministries at the same time, rather than travelling from one institution to the other, across email, text message and voice, with delivery confirmed for each recipient. Indicators from both ministries sit on one dashboard for the epidemiologists who read them. An alert nobody answers escalates on a timer rather than waiting.

A CURRENT DIRECTORY
Who holds the animal health focal point in that province, today?

One directory of positions across human health and animal health, each with a named owner and a named alternate. Validation is built in: holders confirm through the channels and rhythm their institution sets, an unanswered confirmation moves to the alternate, and exercises show who actually answers. Filled against vacant is a live figure rather than an organogram. Workgroups draw their membership from it, with visibility each ministry sets for itself.

LABORATORY CONFIRMATION
Which laboratory can confirm it, and are its results current?

Proficiency testing, equipment calibration and analyst certification are tracked across human, veterinary and environmental laboratories, so a laboratory's current readiness for a method can be checked before the sample is sent. Where the laboratory information system is connected, a confirmed result reaches the officer who sent the sample and the counterpart in the other ministry. The laboratory stays under its own authority, and its records stay in its own system.

ONE CURRENT VERSION
Is the district using the same procedure you approved?

Joint procedures live in one shared workspace, each with a version, a named owner, a review date and an audit trail on every change. When a procedure is revised, the record shows who has acknowledged it and who has not. Neither ministry authors the other's procedures. The joint one is held once and assigned to positions in both.

COMPETENCE RECORDS
Is the officer who took that training still certified?

Certificates are issued, tracked and renewed, with alerts before they expire. When an out-of-compliance event calls for training, the assignment is made automatically and tracked to completion. Training happens wherever it happens. The record exports in the formats accreditation and audit accept, so whether an officer is still certified is a query rather than a search.

CLOSURE ON EVIDENCE
Did the finding from the last exercise get fixed, and by whom?

Every finding is categorised by gap type, assigned to the responsible position rather than a person, and given a deadline that escalates to leadership when it passes. It closes only on uploaded evidence: the revised procedure, the re-verified laboratory panel, the certified roster. Exercises run on the same positions, procedures and alerting as live work, so what they reveal enters the same cycle, and the next exercise tests whether the fix held. A single authority can use this on its own. With a second ministry on the layer, a finding assigned across the boundary still closes.

CONTINUOUS READINESS
If asked today how ready you are, where does the answer come from?

Readiness is scored against targets the country sets for itself: positions filled, procedures current, competence valid, proficiency verified. A scheduled review flags what has lapsed, from an unconfirmed position to a procedure past its review date, before an event finds it. Each figure carries the date it was measured, and reports go on a schedule to the people leadership names.

RECORDS THAT STAY
When a director transfers, what does the successor actually inherit?

Records are bound to the position rather than the person. Procedures, counterparts, decisions and open findings stay attached, so a successor inherits the position with its history rather than a handover conversation.

HOW A COUNTRY BEGINS
A country enters through its most pressing priority, and grows in whatever direction it needs.

Public health is one of seven entry points. A ministry leading on pandemic preparedness, antimicrobial resistance, immunization coordination or zoonotic response anchors the first connection. It is not the only way in. A country whose live problem is aflatoxin in a staple crop or market access after a disease notification enters through food safety or the veterinary mandate and reaches the same place.

Whichever mandate enters first, the second arrives as a peer with its own directory, its own procedures and its own findings, not as a guest inside the first one's system.

Growth then runs in three directions, and none is a prerequisite for the others.

Deeper.

From national and provincial to district, facility and field position. The same directory, the same procedures and the same closure cycle, applied where the work actually happens. A country that simply reaches its districts has made the layer more useful than one that opened three solutions at national level.

Wider.

The other entry points activate on the same platform. A country that begins with public health and later opens food safety does not build a second directory. What is added is that mandate's own procedures and its own findings, which is why the second solution costs less than the first.

Across.

The same mandate in the country next door. A veterinary service working a livestock corridor needs a counterpart position rather than a counterpart person. Because both countries run the same structure, a joint procedure can be held in one version and a cross-border finding can close with evidence in both, without either country's records leaving its own servers.

Where the pressure moves, OACI follows it. That is the adaptation, and it is why none of these is a stage.

No replatforming. Each direction runs on the same OACI Platform, the same security posture and the same accountable data controller, and no ministry is asked to cede authority to another.

What a country commits to at the start is smaller than what is usually asked of it. A named position accountable for the directory being current. A first set of procedures. A date for the first exercise. Everything after that is produced by the work rather than in advance of it.

Nobody has to wait for the system that was planned.
The infrastructure is already running and proven.

Each ministry keeps its own mandate, its own surveillance and its own budget. A briefing starts with where coordination is breaking down now, in whichever mandate is carrying it, not with a programme and not with a priority somebody else set.

What follows is what intentional collaboration can do about it. What OACI brings is the infrastructure and the people who have run it, African incorporated, African controlled.
What you bring is the problem that has to be solved.
